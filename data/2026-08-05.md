### [42] [Measurement of the Higgs boson decay to a low-mass dilepton system and a photon in $pp$ collisions at $\sqrt{s} =$ 13 and 13.6 TeV with the ATLAS detector](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03369)
*ATLAS Collaboration*

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: This paper presents a measurement of the Higgs boson decaying into a photon and a lepton pair using data from the ATLAS detector. A combined analysis shows a signal strength consistent with Standard Model predictions but with varying significance levels.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To measure the decay of the Higgs boson into specific final states (photon and leptons) and to compare the results with Standard Model predictions.

Method: Utilized $pp$ collision data from the ATLAS detector at the LHC with a center-of-mass energy of 13.6 TeV during 2022-2024 with 164 fb$^{-1}$ integrated luminosity, and combined it with previous data from 2015-2018.

Result: The signal strength parameter was measured at $μ= 0.64^{+0.44}_{-0.39}$ for the new data, and $μ= 1.03^{+0.35}_{-0.32}$ when combined with earlier data, both consistent with the expected value of 1.00.

Conclusion: The results indicate a Higgs boson decay into a photon and lepton pair with an observed statistical significance of 3.4 standard deviations after combining both datasets, supporting the Standard Model predictions.

Abstract: A measurement is presented for the Higgs boson decaying into a photon and a pair of electrons or muons with an invariant mass $m_{\ell\ell} <$ 30 GeV, using $pp$ collision data at $\sqrt{s} =$ 13.6 TeV recorded by the ATLAS detector at the Large Hadron Collider during the years 2022-2024, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 164 fb$^{-1}$. The best-fit value of the signal strength parameter, defined as the ratio of the observed signal yield to the one expected in the Standard Model, is measured to be $μ= 0.64^{+0.44}_{-0.39}$ compared with the expected value of $μ= 1.00^{+0.47}_{-0.41}$. The corresponding observed (expected) signal significance is 1.7 (2.5) standard deviations under the background-only hypothesis. The result is combined with a similar search performed using 139 fb$^{-1}$ of $pp$ collision data at $\sqrt{s} =$ 13 TeV collected during the years 2015-2018. This combination gives an observed (expected) signal strength of $μ= 1.03^{+0.35}_{-0.32}\,(μ= 1.00^{+0.34}_{-0.32})$, corresponding to an observed (expected) significance of 3.4 (3.3) standard deviations.
