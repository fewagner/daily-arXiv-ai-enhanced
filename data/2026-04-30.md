### [94] [Resolution Studies for Axion Searches with CUPID-0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25946)
*Livia Petrillo*

Main category: physics.ins-det

TL;DR: This study investigates the potential of CUORE and CUPID detectors to search for high-energy solar axions, revealing a promising energy resolution for detection.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The paper aims to leverage existing detector technology for probing axions, a leading candidate for dark matter and a solution to the strong CP problem.

Method: It performs a resolution study using calibration and background spectra, extrapolating responses in the signal region based on data from CUPID-0 Phase I and II.

Result: The energy resolution FWHM at 5.5 MeV is demonstrated to be (39.8 ± 2.1) keV, facilitating axion searches with low background levels.

Conclusion: The findings indicate that CUORE and CUPID detectors are effective for high-energy solar axion searches due to their excellent energy resolution and low background.

Abstract: Axions, hypothetical particles proposed to solve the strong CP problem and considered promising dark matter candidates, can be produced in the Sun and interact in detectors via couplings to photons, electrons, or nucleons. The CUORE and CUPID scintillating cryogenic calorimeters, originally developed to search for dark matter and neutrinoless double beta decay, are well suited to axion searches due to their excellent energy resolution, particle identification capability and low background. In order to search for high-energy solar axions at 5.5~MeV, a resolution study is carried out using calibration and background spectra, extrapolating the detector response in the hypothetical signal region. The analysis is based on exposures of 9.95~kg$\cdot$yr (CUPID-0 Phase~I) and 5.74~kg$\cdot$yr (CUPID-0 Phase~II). The energy resolution FWHM at 5.5~MeV is found to be $(39.8 \pm 2.1)$ keV for events fully contained in a single crystal, enabling axion searches with a background level lower than $10^{-3}$\,counts/(keV kg y).

### [95] [A new diffuse reflector filament for additive manufacturing of 3D printing finely-segmented plastic scintillator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26033)
*A. Krech,A. Boyarintsev,B. Grynyov,N. Karavaeva,S. Minenko,T. Sibilieva,M. Sibilyev,T. Dieminger,U. Kose,B. Li,A. Rubbia,D. Sgalaberna,T. Weber,J. Wüthrich,X. Zhao,S. Berns,E. Boillat,S. Hugon,A. De Roeck*

Main category: physics.ins-det

TL;DR: This study develops novel white reflective filaments for 3D-printed plastic scintillators, enhancing reflectivity and reducing optical crosstalk.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To create advanced materials for additive manufacturing of scintillator detectors that outperform conventional fabrications in optical properties.

Method: Development of filaments using PC and PMMA loaded with TiO$_2$ and PTFE, evaluation through optical measurements, and testing a prototype with cosmic rays.

Result: The new filaments led to a lower optical crosstalk and higher light yield in 3D-printed scintillators compared to earlier methods.

Conclusion: The study confirms the potential of 3D-printed scintillator detectors with performance on par with traditional detectors, enabled by enhanced filament properties.

Abstract: This study presents the development and the characterization of novel white reflective filaments suitable for additive manufacturing of finely segmented plastic scintillators. The filament is based on polycarbonate (PC) and polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA) polymers loaded with titanium dioxide (TiO$_2$) and polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) to enhance reflectivity. A range of filament compositions and thicknesses was evaluated through optical reflection and transmittance measurements of reflective layers made with the Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) technique. A 3D-segmented plastic scintillator prototype was made with fused injection modeling (FIM) and tested with cosmic rays to assess the light yield and the optical crosstalk. The results demonstrate the feasibility of producing compact and modular 3D-printed scintillator detectors with a performance analogous to standard plastic scintillator detectors. Owing to the improved optical properties of the new reflector filament, a lower light crosstalk and a higher light yield, compared to past works, is obtained.

### [96] [Machine Learning Enables Real-Time Waveform Decomposition for Dual-Readout Calorimetry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26090)
*Liangyu Wu,Qibin Liu,Marco Toliman Lucchini,Julia Gonski,Marcello Campajola,Stefano Moneta*

Main category: physics.ins-det

TL;DR: This paper compares machine learning and template fitting for separating Cherenkov and scintillation signals in dual-readout calorimeters, suggesting ML approaches yield better performance at lower data rates.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To enhance energy resolution in dual-readout calorimeters for next-generation Higgs factories while addressing challenges posed by high data rates in full waveform analyses.

Method: A systematic comparison was conducted between machine learning models and template fitting techniques for signal separation across various crystal types in dual-readout calorimeters.

Result: Machine learning models provided comparable signal extraction performance to traditional methods at lower sampling rates, with a single model effectively handling varying incident particle energies.

Conclusion: The study establishes a baseline for template fitting and presents ML alternatives that improve front-end feature extraction for future crystal-based calorimeter designs.

Abstract: Dual-readout calorimeters achieve superior energy resolution by simultaneously measuring Cherenkov and scintillation signals for event-by-event electromagnetic fraction correction, making them attractive for next-generation Higgs factories. However, if a full waveform readout is required for time-based analysis to separate Cherenkov and scintillation signals, high off-detector data rates might present challenges. These challenges can be mitigated by real-time signal processing in front-end electronics. We present a systematic comparison of machine learning (ML) and template fitting approaches for the separation of scintillation and Cherenkov light components in homogeneous dual-readout calorimeters across three representative crystal types. ML models achieve comparable signal extraction performance at lower sampling rates than template fitting. A single model trained over a range of incident particle energies demonstrates robust performance, and FPGA-compatible compression achieves latencies suitable for real-time application. This work establishes both baseline template fitting performance and ML-enhanced alternatives for crystal-based dual-readout calorimeters, offering practical pathways towards front-end feature extraction in future detector design.

### [97] [Comparison of Silvaco and Synopsys TCAD Predictions Including the Perugia Radiation Damage Model in Silicon Pixel Detectors for the HL-LHC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26386)
*M. Bomben,T. Croci,K. Aouadj,A. Fondacci,F. Moscatelli,A. Morozzi,D. Passeri*

Main category: physics.ins-det

TL;DR: The paper compares device simulation predictions for silicon pixel detectors at the HL-LHC, focusing on radiation-induced performance degradation.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: High radiation fluences at the HL-LHC threaten the performance of silicon pixel detectors, necessitating reliable predictive modeling for operational assessments and algorithm robustness.

Method: The study uses Silvaco and Synopsys TCAD simulations, incorporating the Perugia radiation damage model to analyze surface and bulk defects.

Result: Findings indicate promising predictions for leakage current, depletion voltage, electric field, and trap statistics at specified temperatures and radiation fluences.

Conclusion: The results support improved understanding of detector behavior post-irradiation, aiding operational voltage estimates and tracking algorithm evaluations.

Abstract: At the High Luminosity Large Hadron Collider (HL-LHC), silicon pixel detectors will be exposed to radiation fluences about 5 to 10 times larger than those experienced by the current innermost pixel layers up to today. Signal loss will be the main limitation to tracking and vertexing performance due to radiation damage in hybrid pixel detectors, with the increase in leakage current and depletion voltage posing severe constraints on operating conditions. It is important to have reliable predictions for all observables - such as charge collection performance, leakage current level and breakdown voltage - after irradiation, in order to estimate operational voltage values and to test the robustness of tracking algorithms. In this paper, the predictions of Silvaco and Synopsys TCAD device simulations are compared when the surface and bulk defects and traps of the ``Perugia radiation damage model'' are included. The results are quite promising regarding leakage current, depletion voltage, electric field and trap statistics, at two distinct reference temperatures and fluences.

### [98] [A cm-wave quantum noise limited resonant superconducting parametric amplifier](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26543)
*V. Gilles,T. Sweetnam,B. Mohammadian,M. A. McCulloch,L. Piccirillo*

Main category: physics.ins-det

TL;DR: This study presents a superconducting parametric amplifier (SPA) using a Complementary Split Ring Resonator (CSRR) for narrow-band amplification, achieving high gain and low noise performance.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The need for ultra low-noise amplification in detecting Axion dark matter and in various quantum computing applications drives the development of SPAs.

Method: The amplifier, patterned on a NbTi coated sapphire substrate, operates within four narrow frequency bands in the K band (18-27 GHz) utilizing the kinetic inductance of the superconducting film.

Result: Four resonances were observed between 23.3 and 26.3 GHz with a maximum signal gain of 30 dB and an added noise of 1.2 half quanta at 400 mK.

Conclusion: These findings support advancements in high-frequency Axion dark matter experiments and indicate further exploration into higher frequency applications in quantum technologies.

Abstract: Superconducting Parametric Amplifiers (SPAs) have seen great interest in recent years due to their high gain and quantum limited noise performance. Among these amplifiers, resonant SPAs have been widely developed for experiments where ultra low-noise narrow-band amplification is of interest, such as the search for Axion dark matter in particle physics and the detection of spectroscopic lines in astrophysics, while also finding applications in quantum computing. This work presents an amplifier based on a Complementary Split Ring Resonator (CSRR), patterned on a NbTi coated sapphire substrate embedded within a waveguide, designed to work at a set of four narrow frequency bands throughout K band (18-27 GHz) using the kinetic inductance of the superconducting film. The S-parameters measured at 400 mK, using a sorption cooler, show the four resonances between 23.3 and 26.3 GHz at 1 GHz spacing, with a maximum transmission on resonance of -1 dB. Four-wave mixing has been observed with each resonance, and a maximum signal gain of 30 dB has been measured, corresponding to 29 dB of insertion gain. The noise performance of the amplifier has been measured, showing an added noise of 1.2 half quanta at 400 mK. These results are relevant to high-frequency Axion dark matter experiments and help motivate the exploration of higher frequencies in quantum technologies.
